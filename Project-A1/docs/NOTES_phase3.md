# Phase 3 working notes (Mechanism 2 results, source for REPORT.md)

Data: `data/raw/qos_20261006_070002.csv` (84 configs x 3 shuffled rounds x 5 reps, 0 throttled), placement probes `data/raw/coretype_20261006_0716*.csv`, `..._0717*.csv` and the post-run N=4 probe (newest `coretype_*`). Processed: `data/processed/qos_*.csv`. Plots: `plots/mech2_idle_qos_policy`, `mech2_threads`, `mech2_contention`.

## Verdicts
- **H2a (idle, 1 thread, thread QoS): supported.** Compute 1141 to 1144 M/s and mem 9.35 to 9.39 M/s across all five classes (spread under 0.3%); all classes 99.2% to 100% on Super cores.
- **H2b (idle, process policy): supported.** `-b` and `-c background`: compute 343 to 345 vs 1142 (3.3x slower), DRAM chase 2.87 vs 9.39 (3.3x). Placement 100% Performance cores. IQR/median under 2%. `-c utility` equals plain and stays on Super (control). Caveat: the slowdown is far more than microarchitecture alone explains (see below), so policy also lowers clocks or memory priority; this cannot be separated without PMU or power data.
- **H2c (threads): partly supported.** Default QoS per-thread compute throughput: N=4 1068, N=6 1056, N=12 1069, N=18 1055 vs N=1 1142 (6.5% to 7.6% lower: just outside the predicted 5% for N<=6, inside 10% for N=12 and 18). N=18 threads all at about 188 ms: Performance cores at default policy run the dependent-chain loop as fast as Super cores. N=36: throughput 535, cpu_over_wall 0.50, p99 18.1 ms vs 0.93 ms at N=1 (19x), as predicted.
- **H2d (thread QoS under hog): supported.** cpu_over_wall 0.467 to 0.488 for all five classes, both kinds. Thread QoS is not isolation here.
- **H2e (process policy under hog): supported.** `-b` and `-c background`: cpu_over_wall 0.011 to 0.018, compute throughput 14.8 to 19.1 M/s vs 507 (about 30x lower), p99 chunk 288 to 754 ms vs 19.5 ms under the hog (15x to 39x) and 300x to 800x vs the idle 0.93 ms. The ">100x" prediction holds against the idle baseline, not against the hog baseline; both stated.
- **Unpredicted finding.** Thread QoS background with N>=4 threads is slow: N=4 345 M/s (3.3x lower, IQR up to 794), N=12 178, N=18 115, N=36 56, while default, initiated, interactive and utility stay at about 1060 (N<=18). Probe: 4 background-QoS threads spent 75% of on-core time on Performance cores (default and utility: 99% Super). So thread QoS background does steer placement and speed, but only with several threads; a single background thread on an idle machine stayed on Super. Mechanism is not established: inferred scheduler behavior, no PMU or power evidence.
- **E2x control (QoS applied to main thread after workers start).** Under the hog it is indistinguishable from the correct design (cow 0.47 to 0.49), because thread QoS has no effect there either. A result-only check cannot detect this flaw; only checking `qos_seen` (inside the worker) does. This is the second pitfall write-up for Phase 5.

## Pilot-to-final consistency
The pilot conclusion "thread QoS does nothing" was wrong in general (N>=4 background) and right for N=1 and under the hog. A single-thread, idle-machine design would have led to the false conclusion that QoS is inert: that is the QoS-side AI-resilience story.
